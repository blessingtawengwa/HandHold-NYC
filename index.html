Resources

These links go to outside sites, and Handhold can't see what you do there. Programs, limits, and amounts change, so confirm when you call.

Rent, heat, and groceries when you earn too much for most aid

Many city and state programs have income limits. These places often help at higher incomes, or don't ask about income at all. Ask each one when you call.

  • Your landlord or management company. Call early and ask for a payment plan. If the person who died was the only name on the lease, ask what happens next before you pay or move.
  • Con Edison and National Grid. Ask for a payment plan or a hardship option. Payment plans are often offered without an income test. Ask them to move the account into your name too. Con Edison National Grid
  • Catholic Charities of New York. Emergency help with rent, utilities, and food, open to people of any faith. Ask what's available and whether there is an income limit. Catholic Charities NY
  • Lutheran Social Services of New York. Emergency and family support services. Ask about current programs. LSSNY
  • The Salvation Army. Emergency assistance with rent, utilities, and food varies by location. Salvation Army
  • Food Bank For New York City. Search for a pantry or soup kitchen near you. Many pantries don't ask for proof of income. Food Bank For NYC
  • Your congregation, employer, or union. Many keep hardship or benevolence funds that don't depend on income. Ask your pastor, HR, or union rep. Your employer's Employee Assistance Program may also offer free counseling and referrals.
  • 311. Ask the city to point you to emergency help in your borough. NYC 311

If money is tight: income-based city and state help

These programs have income limits, and a household's income can change a lot after a loss. It costs nothing to check.

  • ACCESS NYC. Answer a few questions and see which city and state benefits you may qualify for. ACCESS NYC
  • NYC Human Resources Administration (HRA). Cash assistance, SNAP food benefits, and emergency one-time help with housing costs. NYC HRA
  • myBenefits NY. Apply online for SNAP, home heating help, and other state benefits. myBenefits
  • HEAP (Home Energy Assistance Program). Help paying heating bills, including emergency heating help. NYS HEAP
  • NY State of Health. If health insurance came through the person who died, you may be able to enroll outside the usual window. Medicaid is also available here. NY State of Health
  • NYS Office of Temporary and Disability Assistance. The state agency behind many of the programs above. NYS OTDA
  • LawHelpNY. A directory of free civil legal help, including housing and benefits problems. LawHelpNY

Pets and animals

If your loved one had a pet, start with food, water, and a safe place today. Then decide on a long-term home when you can.

  • Animal Care Centers of NYC. The city's shelter system. Ask about emergency help and what your options are if no one can keep the pet. NYC ACC
  • ASPCA. Pet-owner resources, and information on keeping a pet in its home when possible. ASPCA
  • Bide-A-Wee. A New York animal welfare organization that helps with adoption and rehoming. Bide-A-Wee
  • Humane Society of New York. Shelter and veterinary services in Manhattan. Humane Society of NY
  • Rehome by Adopt-a-Pet. A tool for finding a new home yourself while meeting the adopter. Adopt-a-Pet
  • Pet food help. Many shelters and rescues run pet food pantries. Ask your vet or one of the groups above. Ask about birds, reptiles, and other animals separately, since they often need specialized care.

Locked phones, computers, and accounts

  • Android phone or tablet. Google can't unlock a screen for family, and a factory reset erases everything. Family can send Google a request about the person's Google account, and Google decides each case and may not release content. Google: deceased user's account Android Help
  • Apple devices and Apple Account. Legacy Contact or a court order. Apple Support
  • Windows PC and Microsoft account. Microsoft Support
